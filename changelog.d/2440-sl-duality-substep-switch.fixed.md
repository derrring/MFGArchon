- **`check_solver_duality` now flags a semi-Lagrangian pair whose halves do not both keep a sub-step within one cell (#2440).** It classified a pair by `_scheme_family` alone, so every `HJBSemiLagrangianSolver` + `FPSLSolver` pair was `DISCRETE_DUAL`. That included a hand-built pair with the HJB half's sub-stepping off and `FPSLSolver`'s default on, which converges in 60 sweeps before #1880's fix and not after (asymmetry 0.95 after 200), and #1880's own mismatch in the other direction.
  - For two instances it now compares whether each half sub-steps with a `cfl_target` of at most 1, and returns `NOT_DUAL` with a warning when they differ, so Expert Mode says so.
  - The count does not matter within one cell: an FP half at `cfl_target=0.45` takes twice the sub-steps with the same stability. A `cfl_target` above 1 does matter: on #1880's fixture an FP half at 4 or 50 was as unstable as one with sub-stepping off. `max_substeps` does not enter, since a step that needs more is refused (#2438).
  - Not compared: an HJB half on the canonical-CS or DPP path, for which no oracle says which FP schedule pairs with it (#2441), and a half that declares no schedule.
  - `create_paired_solvers` builds matched pairs as before. Given an `fp_config` that undoes the match, it now raises naming the conflict, where it built the mismatched pair silently. It also stops writing its defaults into the caller's `hjb_config` / `fp_config`: a reused `fp_config` carried one HJB half's schedule into the next pair.
