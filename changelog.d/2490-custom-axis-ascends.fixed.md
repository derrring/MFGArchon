- **A `TensorProductGrid` axis strictly ascends, and the grid owns it** (#2490, the folded #2748). The custom branch admitted an axis in any order and of any shape, and kept the caller's array, while every reader of an axis takes its index order as its coordinate order. On a descending axis a region marked at x = 1 governed `x_min` and the measure's weights summed to −1; on an axis with one swapped pair the weights stayed positive and integrated wrongly, with nothing raised; and reversing the caller's array after construction, replacing an element of `coordinates`, or unpickling or deep-copying a grid and writing into an axis reordered the grid's axis.
  - An axis is refused by name unless it is 1-D, of its `Nx_points` length and strictly ascending: one that descends, swaps or repeats a node, or is given as a column, is refused, naming the axis, the first node where the order fails and the two coordinates there. A uniform grid whose bounds cannot resolve its points in float64 is refused the same way, naming its bounds and point count.
  - Each axis, custom or uniform, is held as the grid's own read-only float64 copy, and `coordinates` is a tuple. The constructor and `__setstate__` (unpickling, `copy.deepcopy`) are the one place an axis enters, so a pickle holding a non-ascending axis is refused when it is loaded.
  - `CartesianGrid` declares `coordinates: tuple[NDArray, ...]` and its order. The readers that took the attribute off any geometry read it only from a `CartesianGrid`: the region reader, `GridPointMeasure`, `get_spatial_grid`, FP-FDM's node lookups and `ImplicitHeatSolver` refuse another geometry by name, while the face cells are cut without node midpoints and the particle solver takes its bounds from `get_bounds()` and its density axes, for a geometry that is no grid, from those bounds (#2757).
