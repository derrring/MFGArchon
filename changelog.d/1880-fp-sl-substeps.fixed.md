- **The semi-Lagrangian pair no longer amplifies asymmetry when a step's CFL number exceeds 1 (#1880).**
  - **The symptom:** on a reflection-symmetric problem at CFL ~13 (the capability matrix's smoke fixture), SL_LINEAR's coupled Picard map amplified a seeded antisymmetric perturbation ~3.3x per sweep, where upwind FD damps it at 0.89. Picard did not converge, and the density ended against one wall.
  - **The cause:** the two halves structured a step differently. The HJB half cut it into sub-steps; `FPSLSolver` made one forward splat of many cells.
  - **The fix:** `FPSLSolver` now sub-steps exactly when its HJB half does (`HJBSemiLagrangianSolver.substeps_characteristics`), through the function both share, `cfl_substeps`. Each half measures the CFL number on its own velocity and time level. On the fixture the perturbation now decays at 0.98 per sweep, and SL_LINEAR converges in 43 sweeps, symmetric to round-off.
  - **Pairing:** `_create_sl_pair` builds the HJB half first and hands its schedule to the FP half: `enable_adaptive_substepping`, `cfl_target`, `max_substeps`. The mirror mismatch, FP sub-stepping while HJB does not, is as unstable as the original. A hand-built pair must match them too.
  - **Not fixed:** the pair is still not exactly adjoint. The wall gradient stencil and the time level at which each half takes its velocity differ; see the follow-up issue. With the density now concentrating, a 41-point grid re-exposes the NaN after the HJB half's capped-substep warning; it has its own issue.
  - `cfl_substeps` refuses a non-positive `cfl_target`, a `max_substeps` below 1, and a non-finite CFL number.
