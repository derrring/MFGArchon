- **In a multi-population solve `m` is the population's own density, and every population's reaches a density term that declares `cross_density`** (Issue #2335; user ruling 2026-11-06). **Breaking.** `MultiPopulationIterator` handed each population's Hamiltonian the stack of every population's density as `m`, and the library's terms paired it with the points by position: every population read population 0's density, and the FP velocity read node densities at the faces, with no error. Now `m` is the own density at every door, at the faces at the FP velocity, and a density term that reads every population (a `coupling=` or a `congestion_factor=`) declares `cross_density`: one row per population at the same points as `m`, each row with the mass its population was handed, and `m[np.newaxis]` where there is one population. See `CONVENTIONS.md` § *Several populations*.
  - **A body that read the stack from `m`** now gets one population's values. One that branches on `m`'s shape may raise, or may take its other branch with no error: the coupling of the multi-population cross-coupling test, guarded on `m.shape[-1] % K`, lost its cross term that way. Declare `cross_density` on a density term instead.
  - **Only a density term declares `cross_density`.** A Hamiltonian's family method takes `(t, x, p, m)`; the cross term goes in a term. A class whose body defines a family method that declares the slot is refused with `TypeError` when it is created. The check reads the members of the class body by their signature, and `CONVENTIONS.md` lists the forms it does not see. A method of yours overriding one of `SeparableHamiltonian`'s or `CongestionHamiltonian`'s is read at the own density.
  - **Refused before the first sweep, by name, at two or more populations:** a grid of two or more dimensions; node coordinates or time grids (`T`, `Nt`) that differ, compared exactly, which were stacked and read by position before, wrong for every term or failing in numpy; and a Hamiltonian whose term declares `cross_density` while a method whose library body reads its terms is overridden (`CONVENTIONS.md` lists them per class). At one population, a grid of two or more dimensions ends in numpy's broadcast error, as before.
  - **A density term called on a batch that returns neither one value per point nor one value** is refused, saying what it was handed and what it returned, as is a per-point loop handed a density that is neither one value per point nor one value. A `functools.partial` term that froze `cross_density` by keyword is refused where the library would pass it.
