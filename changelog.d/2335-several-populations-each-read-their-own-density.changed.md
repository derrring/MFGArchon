- **In a multi-population solve `m` is the population's own density, and every population's is `cross_density`** (Issue #2335; user ruling 2026-11-06). **Breaking.** `MultiPopulationIterator` handed each population's Hamiltonian the stack of every population's density as `m`, and the library's terms paired it with the points by position: every population read population 0's density, and the FP velocity read node densities at the faces, with no error. Now `m` is the own density at every door, at the faces at the FP velocity, and a term or a Hamiltonian method that reads every population declares `cross_density`: one row per population at the same points as `m`, each row with the mass its population was handed, and `m[np.newaxis]` where there is one population. See `CONVENTIONS.md` § *Several populations*.
  - **A body that read the stack from `m`** now gets one population's values. One that branches on `m`'s shape may raise, or may take its other branch with no error: the coupling of the multi-population cross-coupling test, guarded on `m.shape[-1] % K`, lost its cross term that way. Declare `cross_density` instead.
  - **A method or a term that declares `cross_density` is handed rows, never `None`:** every population's at a multi-population door, and `m[np.newaxis]` everywhere else. A call that hands it none is refused with Python's `TypeError` naming the slot, so a direct call passes `cross_density=m[np.newaxis]` for one population, and a method or an override of a library method passes on what it was handed.
  - **The populations of one solve share one grid:** `MultiPopulationIterator` refuses populations whose node coordinates differ, at two or more populations. Before, they were stacked and read by position, wrong for every term.
  - **A density term that returns neither one value per point nor one value** is refused, saying what it was handed and what it returned, as is a per-point loop handed a density that is neither one value per point nor one value. A `functools.partial` that froze `cross_density` is refused where the library would pass it.
