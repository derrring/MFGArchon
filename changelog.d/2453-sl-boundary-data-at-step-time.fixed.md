- **The semi-Lagrangian HJB solver reads time-dependent boundary data at the step's time (#2453).** The gradient's ghost cells used the gradient operator's default time, t = 0, on the operator-splitting and stochastic paths. The post-step enforcement of operator-splitting sub-steps, of every n-D stochastic step and of the n-D DPP step passed no time either. On a time-dependent Neumann value, `test_hjb_sl_boundary_time_2453`'s cases read at t = 0 before the fix: 1-D ADI 146 of 154 values, 1-D stochastic 30 of 64, and the 2-D cases all of theirs. 1-D DPP already read at its step times. Every read now uses the step's time `time_idx * dt`, the clock #2452 gave H, the foot and the value update.
  - That clock is a decision, not a measured optimum. The step differentiates U^{n+1}, whose own time is t_{n+1}, and no oracle separates the two, because the solver does not hold even constant inhomogeneous Neumann data (#2141). #2453 records the alternative.
  - For boundary data that does not depend on t, U is byte-identical on nine paths.
  - Still open in #2453: an n-D whole step has no post-step enforcement. On the 2-D stochastic and 2-D DPP paths U did not move when the reads did, because the n-D enforcement discards a Neumann value (#2141).
