- **`ImplicitDomain.segment_intervals(a, b)` and `first_contact(a, b)`: the exact parts of a segment inside an implicit domain, and its first boundary contact** (#2733, PR 1). A `Hyperrectangle` solves a slab test, a `Hypersphere` a quadratic, and the CSG nodes combine their children's intervals, a complement entering as the closed set phi >= 0. Each interval end carries the primitive and face that bound it, except where it is the segment's own end, and `first_contact` gives the contact's parameter, point and outward normal. Two surfaces that are one wall in floating point, such as a union's shared face at 0.3 and 0.1 + 0.2, or a ball's end at 0.65 - 0.35, leave each leaf's interval ending at its own surface; such a seam is joined where `contains` holds across it: a gap at most 1024 coarsest ulps wide on every axis, the coarsest ulp being the largest spacing of its ends' coordinates, is read at points no more than one coarsest ulp apart, at its midpoint and at each double of an axis that holds at most 1024, and joined where all hold (#2733 PR 2). A real crack, holding a double outside the domain, stays a gap; one narrower than the segment's parameter resolves, such as 0 | 1e-323, is merged by the union before any join. A one-ulp seam crossed at angle phi to its normal is joined wherever |tan phi| is at most 1024 times the coarsest ulp over the seam's own, which is at least 1024; past that the gap is a wall, unread. Whether a start is inside is decided by the intervals at t = 0, which agree with `contains` there, not by a band around phi = 0. A domain given only by its signed distance is refused by name rather than sphere-traced, since nothing checks that its phi is a distance, and so is a periodic box (#2704). The particle solver's contact loop reads `first_contact` (#2733 PR 2).
