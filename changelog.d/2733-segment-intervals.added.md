- **`ImplicitDomain.segment_intervals(a, b)` and `first_contact(a, b)`: the exact parts of a segment inside an implicit domain, and its first boundary contact** (#2733, PR 1). A `Hyperrectangle` solves a slab test, a `Hypersphere` a quadratic, and the CSG nodes combine their children's intervals, a complement entering as the closed set phi >= 0. Each interval end carries the primitive and face that bound it, except where it is the segment's own end, and `first_contact` gives the contact's parameter, point and outward normal. Two surfaces that are one wall in floating point, such as a union's shared face at 0.3 and 0.1 + 0.2, or a ball's end at 0.65 - 0.35, leave each leaf's interval ending at its own surface; such a seam is joined where `contains` holds across it, read at each double between its two ends on the segment's dominant axis, at most 1024 of them (#2733 PR 2). A real crack, holding a double outside the domain, stays a gap, and a gap of more than 1024 doubles is left as a wall without being read, which a step crossing a seam at a grazing enough angle can reach. Whether a start is inside is decided by the intervals at t = 0, which agree with `contains` there, not by a band around phi = 0. A domain given only by its signed distance is refused by name rather than sphere-traced, since nothing checks that its phi is a distance, and so is a periodic box (#2704). The particle solver's contact loop reads `first_contact` (#2733 PR 2).
