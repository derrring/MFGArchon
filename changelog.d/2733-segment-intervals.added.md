- **`ImplicitDomain.segment_intervals(a, b)` and `first_contact(a, b)`: the parts of a segment inside an implicit domain, solved in closed form per primitive, and its first boundary contact** (#2733, PR 1). A `Hyperrectangle` solves a slab test, a `Hypersphere` a quadratic, and the CSG nodes combine their children's intervals, a complement entering as the closed set phi >= 0. Each interval end carries the primitive and face that bound it, except where it is the segment's own end, and `first_contact` gives the contact's parameter, point and outward normal. A point is in an interval where `contains` holds there, up to rounding near a surface; a `Hypersphere` much smaller than its distance from the segment's start can be missed whole (#2793). Two surfaces that are one wall in floating point, such as a union's shared face at 0.3 and 0.1 + 0.2, or a ball's end at 0.65 - 0.35, leave each leaf's interval ending at its own surface; such a seam is joined where `contains` holds across it: a gap at most 1024 coarsest ulps wide on every axis, the coarsest ulp being the largest spacing of its ends' coordinates, is read and joined where every read holds (#2733 PR 2). The reads cannot see between themselves: a tunnel needs an exterior feature narrower than the read spacing lying between reads, and a seam reflects where its computed gap is wider than the gate. A crack can be joined near a zero coordinate on some axis; a seam reflects where it is crossed at a grazing enough angle, and can near the origin; and a large coordinate on any axis coarsens the gate (#2794). A crack the segment's parameter does not resolve can also be merged by the union before any join. Whether a start is inside is decided by the intervals at t = 0, not by a band around phi = 0; they agreed with `contains` on all 1,246,810 starts it accepts and all 499,258 it rejects in #2791's search (1-D to 3-D, box offsets 0, 1e3 and 1e6; starts on spheres, box faces, edges and corners, two balls' touching point and D6 landings). A domain given only by its signed distance is refused by name rather than sphere-traced, since nothing checks that its phi is a distance, and so is a periodic box (#2704). The particle solver's contact loop reads `first_contact` (#2733 PR 2).
