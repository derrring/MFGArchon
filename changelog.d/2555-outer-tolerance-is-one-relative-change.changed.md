- **The outer coupling tolerance bounds one quantity in every coupling iterator: the relative discrete $L^2$ change one sweep makes, in the problem's own spatial measure** (Issue #2555; `docs/user/CONVENTIONS.md` § 9).
  - **One measure.** `MFGProblem.spatial_measure()` returns how a problem integrates over space, and its name. `initial_mass` and the convergence norm both read it. `initial_mass` is bit-identical before and after on one fixture per branch: grid in 1-D and 2-D, node-sum, uniform-cell in 1-D and n-D, and point-average.
  - **No absolute criterion unless asked for.** `check_convergence_criteria` also required the absolute change below the same tolerance, measured with the first axis's spacing alone, or a unit weight on a mesh. It now compares the relative change, and the absolute one only when `absolute_tolerance` is given: `PicardConfig.absolute_tolerance`, `MFGProblem.solve(absolute_tolerance=)`, and each iterator's `solve` or constructor.
    - `problem.solve()` with its defaults (no-flux, 1-D Nt = 20, 2-D Nt = 10) stops at the same sweep: 21 → 21 in 1-D, 21 → 21 on [0, 1] × [0, 0.6], 20 → 20 on [0, 1] × [0, 10].
    - With the 2-D terminal cost raised by 10 it stops at 18 instead of 20. The absolute criterion was holding the solve two sweeps after its relative change met the tolerance.
    - On a graded grid, `FixedPointIterator`, `BlockIterator` and `FictitiousPlayIterator` no longer raise from `get_grid_spacing()[0]`.
  - **The change is the map's output against its input, in every iterator** (#1684 item 7). `BlockIterator`, `GraphMFGSolver` and `RegimeSwitchingIterator` measured the damped step, `relaxation * (map - old)`, so a smaller relaxation bought the verdict. At relaxation 0.01 on a 12-point fixture they reported 6.1e-03, 2.2e-02 and 1.2e-02, against a map change above 0.69, and a tolerance of 0.1 was met within two sweeps. A solve that stopped on the damped step now runs until the undamped change meets the tolerance.
  - **Multi-field iterators** (`MultiPopulationIterator`, `GraphMFGSolver`, `RegimeSwitchingIterator`) stopped on an absolute max-norm change. Each field's change is now its relative $L^2$ change in its own problem's measure, and the criterion takes the max over fields. `MultiPopulationResult.errors`, `errors_U` and `errors_M`, and the graph and regime `error_history`, now hold these relative changes.
  - `calculate_l2_convergence_metrics(U_map, U_old, M_map, M_old, integrate, dt)` takes the problem's `spatial_measure().integrate` in place of a grid spacing.
