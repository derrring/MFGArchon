- **The particle drift test is seeded, and the issue's diagnosis of why it varied was wrong** (Issue #2352).
  - `test_callable_drift_with_array_diffusion` ran unseeded. #2352 recorded the variation as depending on xdist worker distribution. **Measured: it does not, and cannot.** The ensemble's initial positions come from `RandomState(None)` in `sample_from_density`, and `tests/conftest.py`'s autouse `cleanup_numpy_state` calls `np.random.seed(None)` after **every** test — so nothing is inherited from whatever ran before in the worker.
  - **`np.random.seed()` cannot seed this test**, which is the trap worth recording: a global seed set immediately before the body does not reproduce the run (five runs at one global seed: 0.0263, 0.0289, 0.0221, 0.0251, 0.0235). Only the solver's own `seed=` reaches it — and it reaches **both** paths, the ensemble draw and the Brownian increments: under `seed=7` the run makes zero global `np.random` draws. Bit-identical across repeats and processes, *within a numpy minor version*: `Generator` stream compatibility may break on an X.Y release and `pyproject.toml` pins only `numpy>=2.0`.
  - **Two measurement claims are struck, not one, and the second is the instructive failure.** A first correction struck the test's "30 seeds: 0.02274..0.02718" as understated, then **kept its twin** — `0.00806..0.01372` for the `sigma=0.1` population, measured the same way — and derived the exclusion margin from the survivor. Re-measured over 400 seeds: `0.00724..0.01466`, with **8 of 400 above the struck 0.01372**. The true margin is **22.8%**, not 31%; widening to mean−5σ gives **2.4%**, not 10%. The conclusion survives — the populations still do not overlap — but both numbers carrying the design argument were false. A correction owes the same verification as the thing it corrects.
  - **The band has been observed to fail on both sides, so "zero failures" was never the reason to keep it.** #2352 itself records `0.01738` — *below* the "observed minimum" the first draft published. A 10,000-run sweep hit `0.032648`, above the upper bound. At a fitted per-run failure probability of ~3e-4, P(zero in 3300) = 0.39, so zero-in-3300 is the **expected** outcome of a band that does fail. The band stays wide because the pinned value must survive a numpy `Generator` stream change, not because failures were absent.
  - **What pinning costs, stated because it is not free.** Seed 7 sits at +1.18σ, absorbing a −34% shift before failing where a typical draw absorbs −27%. A `k = 0.7` regression was caught on 69% of unseeded runs and is now never caught. That power was being expressed as intermittent failure — the thing #2352 was filed about — so pinning is the right trade, and a seeded-and-parametrized form would recover it at ~37–55 ms per case.
  - **This closes one instance of a class.** At least five sibling tests in the same file pair an unseeded `FPParticleSolver` with a seed-calibrated numeric band, and 66 of the 79 `FPParticleSolver(` constructions under `tests/` pass no seed. Tracked in #2364; their failure rates are **not** measured here.
