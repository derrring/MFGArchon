- **The FP solvers that read `optimal_control` themselves hand it the step's time and the density they hold** (#2903). FEM and the meshless Galerkin solver read it at $t = 0$ with $m$ = `None`, so a `SeparableHamiltonian` subclass whose own `optimal_control` reads $t$ was solved there as if $t = 0$. The particle solver's three loops read it at the step's time, with $m$ = `None`. So a control that reads $m$ failed on all three solvers: one that uses $m$ directly raised `TypeError`; one that first converts it with `np.asarray(m, dtype=float)` gave a density that was NaN from the first step on FEM and meshless Galerkin, with nothing raised, and raised a `ValueError` on a non-finite particle position on the particle solver.
  - **Now:** for the step from $t_n$ to $t_{n+1}$ each of them hands it $t_n = n\,\Delta t$ and the density it holds at step $n$: FEM at its quadrature points, meshless Galerkin at its nodes, and the particle solver at the particles, its grid density read there, in its CPU loops and its GPU loop alike. That is the convention FVM and the network solver already kept. CONVENTIONS § "What the Fokker–Planck solver receives" states it, with the shapes each solver hands.
  - **`WeakFormFPSolver._build_advection`** now takes `t` and `m_n`, keyword-only and required. An override written as `(U_n, D)` raises `TypeError` at the first step.
  - **Unchanged:** a library Hamiltonian's density, byte for byte, since its control reads neither $t$ nor $m$.
  - **Cost:** reading the density at the particles is new work on every particle solve. Measured as medians, base and head interleaved:
    - default KDE, 10,000 particles: about +10% in 1-D (51 nodes, $N_t = 50$) and +2% in 2-D ($21 \times 21$, $N_t = 20$);
    - `kde_method="cic"`, 100,000 particles, same grids: about +30% to +45% in 1-D and +15% to +30% in 2-D, over three rounds.
