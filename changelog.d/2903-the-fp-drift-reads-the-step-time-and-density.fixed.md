- **The FP solvers that read `optimal_control` themselves hand it the step's time and the density they hold** (#2903). FEM and the meshless Galerkin solver read it at $t = 0$ with $m$ = `None`, and the particle solver's three loops with $m$ = `None`. So a `SeparableHamiltonian` subclass whose own `optimal_control` reads $t$ was solved as if $t = 0$, and one that reads $m$ gave a density that was 150 of 165 entries NaN on FEM and meshless Galerkin, with nothing raised, and raised `ValueError: segment ends must be finite` on the particle solver.
  - **Now:** for the step from $t_n$ to $t_{n+1}$ each of them hands it $t_n = n\,\Delta t$ and the density it holds at step $n$: FEM at its quadrature points, meshless Galerkin at its nodes, and the particle solver at the particles, its grid density read there, in the CPU loops and the GPU loop alike. That is the convention FVM and the network solver already kept; CONVENTIONS § "What the Fokker–Planck solver receives" states it.
  - **Unchanged:** a library Hamiltonian's density, byte for byte, since its control reads neither $t$ nor $m$. Reading the density at the particles costs the particle solver about 10% on a 1-D solve and 2% on a 2-D one.
