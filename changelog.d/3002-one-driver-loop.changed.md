- **One driver loop for the one-component coupled routes** (#3002, stage 2a). `FixedPointIterator`, `FictitiousPlayIterator`, the Block iterators and `NewtonMFGSolver` run their sweeps in `coupling/coupled_driver.py`. Each keeps its constructor and its `solve` signature, and builds the driver with its sweep map, its order between the fields and its damping policy. Newton runs a warm-up phase, then a Newton phase made of the step `NewtonSolver.solve` is made of.
  - **The budget** (the approver's ruling 2):
    - `max_iterations` counts every sweep of a solve, Newton's warm-up included, and 0 runs none. `FixedPointIterator` and `FictitiousPlayIterator` read 0 as unset and ran 48 and 100 sweeps on the measured problem. `None` is each route's default.
    - `newton_max_iterations` bounds Newton's phase within the budget. With a budget below `picard_warmup + 1`, Newton used to run its Newton phase anyway, up to `newton_max_iterations` past the budget: 8 sweeps at budgets 0 to 3 at `picard_warmup=3` on the measured problem. It now stops at the budget.
  - **A spent budget reads `"Maximum iterations reached"`** on every coupled route. The six fixed-point routes read an empty reason, and Newton `"Newton max iterations"`, which it still reads when `newton_max_iterations` runs out with budget to spare.
  - **`FictitiousPlayIterator`'s callback now reads like every other route's** (#2886, the user's ruling 4). Returning `False` stops the solve; returning `True` or `None` continues. It used to be the other way round: `True` stopped and `False` continued. A callback written for the old meaning now does the opposite, so check its return value.
  - **A Newton warm-up sweep makes one HJB solve and one FP solve,** where it made two of each to compute `picard_residuals`, which is removed (the approver's ruling 6).
  - **Every one-component route takes** `iteration_callback`, `track_measure_field` and `track_rate_history` (the approver's ruling 7): the Block iterators and Newton gain the callback; FictitiousPlay, the Block iterators and Newton gain the measure-field record; FixedPoint, the Block iterators and Newton gain the damping-rate record, which FictitiousPlay keeps on.
  - **One progress display,** the hierarchical one FixedPoint showed. The Block iterators' and Newton's `print` lines under `verbose` are gone.
  - **Unchanged, bit for bit:** every route's result under its defaults, and under a budget it spends, on the measured problems. The arrays, the histories and the iteration counts are compared against main at 8598206f (#3002's evidence).
