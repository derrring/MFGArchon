- **FP-FDM's velocity route carries the drift its stencil reads, on every `TensorProductGrid`** (Issue #2904). For a Hamiltonian that is not smooth and separable (an L1 or bounded control cost, congestion, a Hamiltonian of your own), the coupling layer hands FP-FDM `drift_field=compute_fp_velocity_field(...)`. That function built a field other than the one FP-FDM's divergence-upwind stencil reads. On a periodic 1-D grid the face across the wrap read the endpoint the grid repeats, where the stencil reads node 0. On a 2-D or 3-D grid it returned node values of centred differences, which the stencil reads as face values. `problem.solve()` then returned another density with no error. On the quadratic control cost, whose drift both routes carry, the velocity route was 7.790 apart in M from the value-function route on a 2-D periodic grid of 9 × 9 nodes, and 14.81 on a 3-D periodic grid of 5³.
  - **Now the velocity is built at the stencil's faces,** along each axis, on the FP solver's own BC. The normal component of `p` is the forward difference across the face, each other component the mean of the two nodes' centred differences, and `m` the mean of the two nodes. On the quadratic cost the two routes are equal to round-off on no-flux, Dirichlet, periodic and mixed walls, in 1-D, 2-D and 3-D, with a constant or a field volatility. On an axis that does not wrap and has three nodes or more, the transverse components of `p` are second-order accurate at a face, so a smooth control that reads every component of `p` converges at second order there. See `CONVENTIONS.md` § *What the Fokker–Planck solver receives*.
  - **In nD the array keeps its shape** `(Nt, d, *grid shape)`. Entry `[d][i]` is now the velocity at the face `(i + 1/2)` along `d`; the last node of an axis that does not wrap carries 0, and a node a wrapping axis repeats carries its twin's face. On a `TensorProductGrid` the control is asked only at faces inside the domain; another geometry's layout is #2910's.
  - **`compute_fp_velocity_field` reads the walls it builds for through the grid's check.** It takes `boundary_conditions=`, the FP solver's BC, which the coupling layer passes. Handed none, it reads the problem's shared BC as an FP solver reads it (`fp_view_of_shared_bc`), where before it read no BC. On a `TensorProductGrid` the grid's coordinates own the periodic layout: through the check it binds every BC with (`bind_periodic_convention`), an unstated convention takes the grid's and a stated one that contradicts the grid is refused with the grid's message. FP-FDM's own route keeps such a convention and solves, which is #2910's. The solvers' own completion now has one owner too, `with_geometry_periodic_convention`.
    - A direct call on a problem whose shared BC has a Neumann value that is not zero, or a Robin condition, now raises `NotImplementedError`, as FP-FDM on that problem does.
    - A BC periodic on some faces only raises `NotImplementedError`, as FP-FDM refuses it (#2495), where the builder built as if no axis wrapped.
  - **An own optimal control** is taken as a velocity on the closed set of #2900, which #2908 widens to periodic and Dirichlet walls and 2-D and 3-D grids. Before #2908, routing periodic walls and 2-D grids, where the routes now agree, is #2908.
