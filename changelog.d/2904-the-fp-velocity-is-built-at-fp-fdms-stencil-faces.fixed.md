- **FP-FDM's velocity route carries the drift its stencil reads, on every grid** (Issue #2904). For a Hamiltonian that is not smooth and separable (an L1 or bounded control cost, congestion, a Hamiltonian of your own), the coupling layer hands FP-FDM `drift_field=compute_fp_velocity_field(...)`. That function built a field other than the one FP-FDM's divergence-upwind stencil reads. On a periodic 1-D grid the face across the wrap read the endpoint the grid repeats, where the stencil reads node 0. On a 2-D or 3-D grid it returned node values of centred differences, which the stencil reads as face values. `problem.solve()` then returned another density with no error. On the quadratic control cost, whose drift both routes carry, the velocity route was 7.790 apart in M from the value-function route on a 2-D periodic grid of 9 × 9 nodes, and 14.81 on a 3-D periodic grid of 5³.
  - **Now the velocity is built at the stencil's faces,** along each axis, on the FP solver's own BC. The normal component of `p` is the forward difference across the face, each other component the mean of the two nodes' centred differences, and `m` the mean of the two nodes. On the quadratic cost the two routes are equal to round-off on no-flux, Dirichlet, periodic and mixed walls, in 1-D, 2-D and 3-D, with a constant or a field volatility. A control that reads every component of `p` converges at second order at the faces. See `CONVENTIONS.md` § *What the Fokker–Planck solver receives*.
  - **In nD the array keeps its shape** `(Nt, d, *grid shape)`. Entry `[d][i]` is now the velocity at the face `(i + 1/2)` along `d`, and the last node of an axis that does not wrap carries 0. On a 1-D grid that wraps with no repeated node, the array has one face more, across the wrap.
  - **An own optimal control** is still taken as a velocity only on the closed set of #2900. Routing periodic walls and 2-D grids, where the routes now agree, is #2908.
