- **A `SeparableHamiltonian` that serves an optimal control of its own now drives the FP equation with it, or the solve refuses** (Issue #2900). Handed the value function, FP-FDM and the semi-Lagrangian solver formed the drift as $-c\,\nabla u$, the control cost's. So on `problem.solve()`'s default scheme a subclass that redefined $H$ solved with its base class's drift, with no error: 1.2074e+00 off in $M$ from the library `SeparableHamiltonian` for the same $H$, on the fixture of `test_fp_drift_from_optimal_control_2900.py`.
  - **Which Hamiltonians:** every `SeparableHamiltonian` not exactly as the library ships it (#2914): a class other than `SeparableHamiltonian` or `QuadraticMFGHamiltonian`, a cost other than a `QuadraticControlCost`, or a family method held on either instance.
  - **Routed:** FP-FDM takes its `optimal_control` as a velocity on a closed set: a tensor grid whose every face is no-flux, periodic, Dirichlet or a prescribed flux, handed the density trajectory, where FP-FDM reads the velocity (#2908, #2915). That is the default scheme there, each node of a `GraphMFGSolver` solve, and each population of a `MultiPopulationIterator` solve, read at its own density (#2335). It solves as the library Hamiltonian it describes.
  - **Refused:** FP-FDM off that set, the semi-Lagrangian solver, HJB-FDM's `build_advection_matrix`, FP-FDM's `build_advection_operator` (and so `BlockIterator(adjoint_verify=True)`, which checks against it), and the meshless HJB's streamline diffusion. Off the set means a geometry that is not a tensor grid, several populations above 1-D, or `RegimeSwitchingIterator`, which hands no trajectory. FP-FDM accepts walls of the four kinds above only, so no other wall kind reaches the set. On the set, a solve whose step reads no velocity (the tensor-diffusion path of a tensor volatility, in every dimension FP-FDM builds it, or a scheme other than `divergence_upwind` such as `FDM_CENTERED`) is refused by FP-FDM's #1632 guard at the first FP solve, after one HJB solve. On a `TensorProductGrid`, a periodic boundary condition whose stated convention contradicts the grid is refused there too, with the grid's message, through the velocity builder (#2910). Each of these refusals states what was run and on what, and why it refuses, and points to CONVENTIONS § "What the Fokker–Planck solver receives", which describes the solvers that read `optimal_control` themselves and what each one refuses. It names no other scheme; FP-FDM's #1632 guard, which refuses a velocity its step would not read, names the scheme that reads one.
  - **Unchanged:** FVM, the particle solver and the weak-form family read `optimal_control` themselves and keep the value-function route. The particle solver hands it $m$ = `None`, and the weak-form family also $t = 0$ (#2903). A library Hamiltonian with a `QuadraticControlCost` keeps its route.
  - **Now solved:** a `SeparableHamiltonian` whose control cost is smooth and not a `QuadraticControlCost`, such as `L1ControlCost(...).regularize(...)` or a cost of the user's own. The default scheme refused it, since $-c\,\nabla u$ is defined only for a quadratic cost (#1542); on the set above it now takes its `optimal_control` as a velocity. The exact case: a quadratic cost written as a `ControlCostBase` subclass now solves as the library `QuadraticControlCost` of the same $\lambda$, 8.9e-16 apart in $M$ in 1-D and 1.8e-15 in 2-D.
