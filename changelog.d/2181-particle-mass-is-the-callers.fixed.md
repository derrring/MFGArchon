- **The particle FP solver returned mass 1 whatever the caller handed in, contradicting the mass the library reports** (Issue #2181). `MFGProblem` publishes `problem.initial_mass` and no longer rescales the caller's density (#1887); the particle solver then returned a density integrating to 1 regardless, a factor of **3.33** on a deliberate 0.3-mass population. **The mechanism is not what the issue first said, and the fix it first proposed would have done nothing.** `kde_normalization` is not the cause: measured, `NONE` gives mass 1.0 as well, and instrumentation shows `_should_normalize_density()` returning `False` on every call with `_normalize_density` never invoked. The mass is destroyed by the **particle representation itself** — `sample_from_density` draws N particles from the density's *shape*, a set of positions carries no scale, and the KDE reconstruction returns ≈1 by construction. So the quantity was not rescaled but **absent**, and had to be reintroduced deliberately. `solve_fp_system` now measures the caller's mass with `geometry.integrate` (#2145, so the solver preserves the same functional the library reports), calls the unchanged implementation, and scales the returned history by one constant. That scale is **exact, not an approximation**: within one call the drift field is given, so the FP step is linear in `m` and the solution for `c·m₀` is `c` times the solution for `m₀` — which also makes it one owner instead of a correction repeated in the 1-D, GPU, nD and callable-drift paths. Multi-population shares now survive this solver: two populations built for 0.3 / 0.7 come back at 0.300000 / 0.700000 summing to 1.000000, where both previously read 1.0 and summed to 2.0. `kde_normalization` keeps its own meaning and gains the one it should always have had — `NONE` leaves the KDE's drift visible at the caller's scale, `INITIAL_ONLY` pins t=0, `ALL` pins every step — rather than all three meaning "the mass is 1". Five tests asserted `mass == 1` and now assert drift from the initial mass, which is what conservation means and is invariant to a modelling choice #1887 made the caller's: the same correction #1672 applied to `SolverResult.mass_conservation_error`.
