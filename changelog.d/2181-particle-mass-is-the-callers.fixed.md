- **The particle FP solver returned mass 1 whatever the caller handed in, contradicting the mass the library reports** (Issue #2181). `MFGProblem` publishes `problem.initial_mass` and no longer rescales the caller's density (#1887); the particle solver then returned a density integrating to 1 regardless, a factor of **3.33** on a deliberate 0.3-mass population. **The cause is not `kde_normalization`, and changing its default would have done nothing:** measured, `NONE` gives mass 1.0 as well, with `_normalize_density` never invoked. A particle method carries no mass — `sample_from_density` draws N particles from the density's *shape*, positions have no scale, and the reconstruction returns whatever constant its kernel conserves. The quantity was **absent**, not rescaled. `FPParticleSolver._measure` becomes the one place that answers "what is this slice's mass", always on `geometry.integrate`'s measure, and `_to_caller_mass` the one place that puts the caller's mass back — pinning a slice to the target when `kde_normalization` asks for the correction, otherwise multiplying by one factor calibrated on the first slice of the solve so the reconstruction's own drift stays visible. It runs at **each reconstruction**, before the density reaches the drift callable: scaling the returned history instead rests on the FP step being linear in `m`, which is false when the drift depends on the density (`drift_needs_density` defaults to `True`; measured 85–102% relative error). `_compute_total_mass` and the `normalize_density` import are deleted as the change orphaned them, and the `particle_mass_counting_measure` discrimination mutation is retargeted onto `_measure`, whose convention it now defends. **`kde_normalization` means what its names say for the first time**, and the modes are distinguishable on every KDE method (ALL−NONE: reflection 8.0e-03, cic 7.7e-03, standard 4.3e-02); previously all three meant "the mass is 1". Multi-population shares survive this solver: 0.3 / 0.7 return 0.300000 / 0.700000 summing to 1.000000, where both read 1.0 and summed to 2.0. **Found on the way, and not fixed here:** `KDEMethod.CIC` documents "exact mass conservation" and its raw reconstruction integrates to exactly `n/(n-1)` — 1.100000 at n=11, 1.050000 at n=21, 1.025000 at n=41 — because it deposits into CELLS while an endpoint-inclusive grid has one more node than cells. It conserves a constant, not 1; the calibration absorbs it, and the false docstring is tracked separately. Nine test assertions pinned the representation's loss and now assert drift from the initial mass or compare against the input; two absolute bounds on a density were rescaled by the caller's mass, one in an `optional_torch` file that every marker set deselects.
